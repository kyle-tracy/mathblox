# Characters: who you play (commissioner direction, 2026-10-23)

Pressing a key many times in a row is the problem. Each character has a different way of moving. When a new character is introduced, you get a short piece of true trivia, imperfections included. The game should feel full of people, color and history, not sterile.

Draft roster (CEO). Matho fact-checks the trivia and Worldo designs levels around each mover.

| Character | Movement | Intro trivia (imperfections kept) | Art cue |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Tally-Keeper** (starter) | 1 step | The Ishango bone from the Congo is about 20,000 years old and is notched in groups. It may be one of the oldest counting tools; nobody knows for sure. | bone, ochre, cave-wall hands |
| **Euclid** | slides until something stops him | *Elements* was taught for about 2,000 years, yet we know almost nothing about Euclid's life. His very first proof assumes two circles cross, which his own rules never guarantee. | red-figure pottery, papyrus |
| **Pythagoras** | knight's jump, exactly √5 away (the long side of a 1-2 triangle) | Babylonian scribes listed right-triangle numbers (Plimpton 322) more than 1,000 years before him. His followers wouldn't eat beans. Legend says one of them was drowned for revealing irrational numbers; that story is probably made up. | temple columns, beans |
| **Brahmagupta** | negation: every move goes the opposite way | In 628 CE he wrote the first rules for zero and for negative numbers, calling them "debts" and "fortunes". He also said 0÷0 = 0, which was wrong. | Rajasthani miniature palette |
| **Zeno** | goes half of the way to the wall each move | His paradoxes argued that motion is impossible, since to arrive you first have to go halfway, and halfway again, and so on. | stoa, tortoise and arrow |
| **Fibonacci** | moves 1, 1, 2, 3, 5… steps, longer each time | He brought Hindu-Arabic digits to Europe in 1202. "His" sequence had been described by Indian scholars (Pingala, Hemachandra) centuries earlier. | Pisa marble, rabbits |
| **al-Khwarizmi** | repeats a move sequence you record (an algorithm) | "Algebra" comes from *al-jabr*, his word for restoring, a term also used for setting bones, and "algorithm" comes from his name. | Baghdad tilework, astrolabe |
| **Noether** | moves, and a mirror twin moves opposite (Visio's Mirror Twin) | She proved that every symmetry comes with a conservation law, and for years she taught at Göttingen without pay. | Göttingen chalkboard |

Principles:
- Where possible, the way a character moves *is* their math. Euclid's is a line extended, Pythagoras's is a hypotenuse, Brahmagupta's is negation.
- More entities: characters, creatures, people from history wandering the Craft world. Color everywhere.
- Every level should be fun. Each character's levels force a thought that only that character can produce.
